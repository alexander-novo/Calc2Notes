Solution

Recall the Pythagorean Identity sin2(𝑥)+cos2(𝑥)=1, rearranging this we can write cos2(𝑥)=1−sin2(𝑥). Then using this in the integral we have

∫cos3(𝑥)d𝑥=∫cos2(𝑥)cos(𝑥)d𝑥=∫(1−sin2(𝑥))cos(𝑥)d𝑥

This might look more complicated at first, but now if we tried a 𝑢-sub it will work! Let 𝑢=sin(𝑥), then d𝑢=cos(𝑥)d𝑥, so the integral becomes

∫(1−sin2(𝑥))cos(𝑥)d𝑥=∫1−𝑢2d𝑢=𝑢−𝑢33=sin(𝑥)−sin3(𝑥)3

dd𝑥sin(𝑥)−sin3𝑥3=cos(𝑥)−sin2(𝑥)cos(𝑥)=cos(𝑥)(1−sin2(𝑥))=cos(𝑥)cos2(𝑥)=cos3(𝑥)

Warning

In this section and on its WebAssign problems you may have to use other trigonometric identities. It is good to remember these exist every now and then, and practice using them, but having you memorize too many of these is not worth your time and energy.

You are expected to know the Pythagorean identity and alternative versions of it, but any other identity you need you may look up. Examples include the half angle and double angle formulas, and product to sum formulas, etc.

Notice in the above problem that in our 𝑢-sub, it was nice to have everything in terms of 1 trig function (sin(𝑥) in the above example), except a single multiple of the other trig function times d𝑥 (cos(𝑥) in the above example). Then the first variable can be our 𝑢. We will repeat this technique below, getting rid of most of one trig function except something to work for d𝑢. This takes a lot of practice and trial and error to get good at, make sure to try things and be okay failing and trying again.

Because we need 1 trig function left over for d𝑢, and the power of sin(𝑥) is odd, we can leave 1 sin(𝑥) alone, and replace the other 4 powers with cos(𝑥) through the Pythagorean identity.

Solution

When we use double andgle formula, we might have to simplify a lot and try the formula again.

∫sin2(𝑥)cos2(𝑥)d𝑥=∫12(1−cos(2𝑥))12(1+cos(2𝑥))d𝑥=14∫1−cos2(2𝑥)d𝑥

Then we have to use double angle formula again.

∫sin2(𝑥)cos2(𝑥)d𝑥=14∫1−cos2(2𝑥)d𝑥=14∫1−12(1+cos(4𝑥))d𝑥=14∫1−12−cos(4𝑥)d𝑥

Then let 𝑢=4𝑥, d𝑢=4d𝑥 so

∫sin2(𝑥)cos2(𝑥)d𝑥=14∫1−12−cos(4𝑥)2d𝑥=14∫12d𝑥−132∫cos(𝑢)d𝑢=18𝑥−132sin(4𝑥)+𝐶.

Tip

Strategy for evaluating ∫sin𝑚(𝑥)cos𝑛(𝑥)d𝑥

  • Case 1: At least one of 𝑚 or 𝑛 are odd:

    • Choose the trig function with the odd power to be 𝑢. If both are odd, just pick one.
    • Save one factor of this trig function for d𝑢 later.
    • Rewrite the other trig function (even power) with the Pythagorean Identity.
    • Do 𝑢-sub, expand everything, and evaluate.

  • Case 2: Both 𝑚 and 𝑛 are even

    • Reduce all even powers with the double angle identities.
    • You may need to expand and apply the previous step multiple times until all even powers are eliminated.
    • The identity sin(2𝜃)=2sin(𝜃)cos(𝜃) may sometimes be useful.

Tip

Strategy for evaluating ∫tan𝑚(𝑥)sec𝑛(𝑥)d𝑥

  • Case 1: 𝑛 even: (i.e. the power of secant is even)

    • Save one factor of sec2(𝑥) for d𝑢 later.
    • Rewrite the rest of the powers of sec2(𝑥) using sec2(𝑥)=1+tan2(𝑥) (even power) with the Pythagorean Identity.
    • Do 𝑢-sub, expand everything, and evaluate.

  • Case 2: 𝑚 is odd: (i.e. the power of tangent is odd)

    • Save one factor of sec(𝑥)tan(𝑥) for d𝑢 later.
    • Convert the remaining even powers of tangent to tan2(𝑥)=sec2(𝑥)−1
    • Do 𝑢-sub, expand everything, and evaluate.

∫tan(𝑢)d𝑢=ln|sec(𝑢)|+𝐶∫sec(𝑢)d𝑢=ln|sec(𝑢)+tan(𝑢)|+𝐶
